You **absolutely can**. In fact, I think that makes the business model stronger—provided we structure it correctly.

The distinction is between **owning/performing every trade yourself** and **being the single operating company responsible for coordinating and delivering all post-handover services**.

# The better model

Instead of saying:

> "We are a property management company."

Position it as:

> **"We operate the property after handover—from maintenance and repairs to leasing, interiors, resident services and long-term asset management."**

That gives you a much broader business.

# 12. But There Is One Important Trap

**Don't become a giant company employing thousands of plumbers, electricians and carpenters too early.**

Instead, initially build:

> **Managed service network + internal quality control.**

For example:

```text
                 YOUR COMPANY
                      │
               Operations Team
                      │
        ┌─────────────┼─────────────┐
        │             │             │
    Plumbing      Electrical     Carpentry
    Partners       Partners       Partners
        │             │             │
        └─────────────┼─────────────┘
                      │
                   PROPERTY
```

You control:

* Customer relationship
* Pricing
* SLA
* Quality
* Scheduling
* Work orders
* Inspection
* Payment
* Warranty
* Customer support

The contractor executes the physical work.

As your volume increases, you can selectively bring high-volume services in-house.
